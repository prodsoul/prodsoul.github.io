Moustach’ve Been the Wind

I led an eight-person team building Moustach’ve Been the Wind, a 2D speedrunning platformer about an octopus barber racing the clock. I worked on movement, level design and the handoff from Unity to our custom engine.

Date
September 2025 – April 2026
Project type
Team project
My role
Project manager and game designer
Methods used
Movement prototyping, Unity, level design, playtesting

Game trailer

Designing routes that reward momentum

Skid turns, wall kicks and a ground pound that carries into a roll give the player ways to redirect speed. Running also slows the clock’s drain. I prototyped and tested movement in Unity with a programmer and documented the inputs and level requirements.

In a blockout, I could check where the player had room to build speed and where a turn or landing would interrupt a run. The routes gave those movement decisions a place to matter.

Moving tested levels into the custom engine

I built a JSON export workflow that carried the tested tile layouts, layers and collision from Unity into the custom engine. That preserved the routes we had already tested and let us compare the same stretch of level before and after the transition.

The final gameplay

I managed milestones and coordinated level and audio decisions. The programmers developed the custom engine and our art teammate created the trailer. The gameplay shows the final movement and routes together.
