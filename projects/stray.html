Stray Game Design Analysis

I studied how Stray makes movement and small optional actions feel specific to playing as a cat. I presented the analysis in an interactive Figma report.

Date
November 2024
Project type
Solo analysis
My role
Game design analyst and interaction designer
Methods used
Gameplay observation, interaction analysis, Figma

From input to feedback

I observed exploration and jumping alongside meowing, scratching and knocking objects over. I compared what the player does with the animation, sound or controller feedback that follows.

Why optional actions matter

Movement sets the rhythm of exploration. Small actions give players room to express themselves within that space. The report uses those examples to discuss agency, embodiment and flow through actions a player can actually try.

Designing how the report is read

I used the game’s startup sequence and chapter menus to structure the report. Readers can choose where to start and move between the introduction, findings and conclusion. I created the analysis, writing and Figma interface. The gameplay and screenshots come from BlueTwelve Studio’s Stray.
