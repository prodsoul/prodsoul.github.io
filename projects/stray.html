Stray Game Design Analysis

November 2024 Playtesting & Analysis Gameplay observation, interaction analysis, Figma Solo analysis · Game design analyst and interaction designer

I studied how Stray makes ordinary actions feel specific to playing as a cat. I documented what players do and how the game responds, then designed an interactive Figma report so readers could explore the findings by chapter.

Read Report Here

Start with the action and its response

I looked at jumping and exploration alongside optional actions such as meowing, scratching, and knocking objects over. For each example, I tracked the input and the response, from a movement animation to a sound or controller cue. That gave me something concrete to look at when explaining why an interaction felt physical.

Look at what players choose to do along the way

Jumping helps players move through the environment. The optional actions let them decide how to spend time in it. A player can stop to scratch or knock something over even when it does nothing to advance the story. I used that difference to discuss agency and what makes the experience feel like playing as a cat.

Turn the analysis into a report readers can explore

I used the startup sequence and chapter navigation as references for the report’s structure. The chapters give readers a way to jump to the examples they care about and return to the argument. I wrote the analysis and built the Figma interface. The game and its screenshots are from BlueTwelve Studio’s Stray.

What I’d look at in a follow-up

I’d watch new players explore the same area and note which optional interactions they notice or repeat. That would give me a way to compare my own observations with what other players do when they’re free to choose their route.
