Stray Game Design Analysis

I analysed how Stray makes familiar actions feel specific to playing as a cat. I presented the analysis as an interactive Figma report modelled on the game’s startup sequence and chapter selection.

Date
November 2024
Project type
Solo analysis
Organisation
DigiPen Institute of Technology
My role
Game design analyst and interaction designer
Methods used
Gameplay observation, interaction analysis, Figma

Studying the player’s actions

I looked at movement and exploration alongside small optional actions such as meowing, scratching and knocking objects over. For each one, I traced what the player does and how the game responds through animation, sound or controller feedback.

What made the cat feel playable

Jumping and exploring shape how players move through a space. Actions like scratching and meowing let players express themselves while they are there. I used those examples to connect individual inputs to the report’s broader discussion of agency, embodiment and flow.

Presenting the findings

I used the startup and chapter menus to let readers move through the report in a familiar sequence. The analysis, writing and Figma prototype are my work. Screenshots and gameplay excerpts are from BlueTwelve Studio’s Stray.
