Netflix Mobile Discovery

I researched how people choose what to watch on Netflix and designed an Explore flow for viewers who have a rough idea of what they want. I wanted them to be able to narrow their options and compare a few titles without leaving the app.

Find out where choosing gets difficult

I surveyed ten Netflix viewers and compared four streaming apps. In my survey, ease of finding something to watch averaged 2.4 out of 5. The journeys I mapped showed two places where a viewer could get stuck: narrowing a catalogue that still felt too broad, and opening other apps to check whether an individual title was worth watching.

Let viewers combine what matters to them

I worked through the browsing route on paper before building it in Figma. In the concept, Explore replaces New & Hot as the place to look for something specific. Filters sit in expandable groups so someone can combine criteria, such as a genre and a language, before looking at the results.

Put comparison details where people browse

I added age ratings and Netflix user scores to the poster cards. On the details page I kept the Netflix scores separate from external reviews, so viewers could tell what each number meant. The poster helps with a quick comparison, while the details page has room to explain the title and its ratings.

What the prototype feedback showed

People who reviewed the prototype pointed to the combined filters and ratings on results as useful changes. If I tested it again, I’d give viewers the same viewing brief in both versions and watch how they narrow the list, where they look for reassurance, and when they settle on a title.
