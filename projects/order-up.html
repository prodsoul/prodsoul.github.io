Order Up!

  • March 2025
  • Playtesting & Analysis
  • Paper playtesting, rules writing, Illustrator
  • Team project · Game design, prototyping and print production

Order Up! is a real-time card game for three to five players. I made paper prototypes, drafted the first rulebook, and assembled the final card layouts for print using my teammates’ artwork.

Build pressure around a shared pantry

Everyone swaps dish cards with the same pantry at the same time. Completing a set gives a player the option to use a chef ability, and completing four sets ends the round. The pantry changes while players are looking at it, so they have to decide whether to grab a useful card now or wait for a better one.

Run the playtest at the intended pace

We tried several paper concepts before settling on Order Up! In the first sessions we introduced the rules one turn at a time. That helped us explain individual actions, but it gave us little insight into what would happen when everyone reached for the same cards.

I moved toward more focused tests at real-time pace, looking at when players tried to use abilities and which effects made the table stop. That helped us identify timing rules people were unsure about during the game.

Make the printed game teach itself

I drafted the instructions and assembled the final Illustrator layouts. In print, the cards, box, and rules had to work together without someone from the team explaining the setup. Giving the rules to people unfamiliar with the game helped us see where we had assumed too much.

What I’d test next

I’d give a fresh group a copy and watch their first round without stepping in. I’d note where they stop swapping cards, reach for the rulebook, or disagree about when an ability resolves.
