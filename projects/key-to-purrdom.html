I designed most of the puzzles and levels for Key to Purrdom, a cat-themed 2D puzzle platformer. I also implemented gameplay and integrated audio in Unity while my art teammates created the visual assets.

Show what each mouse controls

Interacting with a colour-coded mouse toggles the platforms that match it. I used early rooms to give players room to try one mouse and see which platforms move. That connection needs to be easy to spot before a room asks them to keep track of several colours.

Build the puzzles around a sequence

Once the relationship is familiar, players have to work out which platforms they need at each point in the route. A platform that helps them get to one place can be in the way later, and the order in which they use the mice affects what they can reach.

Watch where a player hesitates

In classroom playtests I paid attention to players who could see where they needed to go but paused before opening the route. Watching what they looked at and what they tried helped me distinguish an unclear mouse-to-platform connection from a puzzle that simply needed more thought.

What I took into the final game

I built the interactive levels and integrated audio in Unity. If I tested the game again, I’d ask a new player to predict what one mouse will change before trying it, then see whether they can use that relationship on their own in a later room.
