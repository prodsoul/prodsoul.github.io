Amazon Search & Prime

  • October 2025
  • Digital Product UX
  • Heuristic evaluation, paper prototyping, Figma
  • Team concept redesign · Search filters and membership designer

In a four-person concept project, I redesigned Amazon’s mobile search filters and Prime membership flows. I worked on making applied filters easier to review and making the choice between Prime plans easier to understand.

Identify what shoppers had to piece together

In our review of the app, clearing or changing a filter could mean reopening a panel to work out which values were active. Prime information also appeared in several places without an easy way to compare plans together. I took those two flows from paper sketches into low-fidelity wireframes and high-fidelity screens while my teammates worked on other shopping and account flows.

Keep the active filters visible

I reduced the quick-filter row to four entry points and moved the full list into a filter sheet. Within it, selected values rise to the top and active groups are marked. Shoppers can clear an individual group, which lets them remove a constraint without rebuilding the rest of their search.

Give the membership choice one place to happen

I brought monthly and annual plans together so shoppers could compare prices and benefits before joining. For existing members, I designed a separate view that brings their savings and benefits into the membership flow.

What I took into the final concept

Feedback on the wireframes highlighted the filter panel and seeing Prime information together. If I tested the concept again, I’d ask shoppers to narrow a product search, change one filter, then compare the membership plans. I’d look at whether they can say which filters are still active and explain the difference between the plans.
