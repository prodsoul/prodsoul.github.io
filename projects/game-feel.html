Game Feel in Unity

Month · Year Game & Level Design Unity, HUD design, camera, audio feedback Solo prototypes · Interaction designer and Unity prototyper

I built two solo Unity prototypes around game feel. Last Token uses an abandoned carnival’s objects to tell players what’s happening in combat. Zoomies uses a low camera and sound to give a short run through a park the energy of a dog sprinting.

Download Last Token Here Download Zoomies Here

Last Token — show what the player can do

I used gumballs to show ammo and carnival tickets to distinguish light, medium, and heavy attacks. Gumballs disappear as the player fires and return on reload. That ties the counter to an action the player can see, and the ticket cues tell them which attack they’re about to use.

Last Token — show whether the action landed

A player needs different cues for selecting an attack and successfully landing one. I paired attacks with sound and impact feedback, so the player has something to check at the point of contact instead of looking back at the HUD.

Zoomies — work from a dog’s eye level

I built the park sprint around a low first-person camera, quick movement, and sound that responds to the run. From that height, familiar objects feel closer and pass more quickly. The route gives players open space to pick up speed and nearby scenery to judge how fast they’re moving.

What I learned from building both

In Last Token, I needed feedback that could answer a question during combat: how much ammo is left, or did the hit land? In Zoomies, I used feedback to give the same space a different sense of speed and scale. If I revisited the two builds, I’d watch players with the HUD and audio cues reduced one at a time to see which cues they rely on.
