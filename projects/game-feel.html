Game Feel in Unity

I built two Unity prototypes to explore how feedback changes the feel of play. One makes combat state readable through a carnival-themed HUD. The other puts the player at a dog’s eye level for a fast run through a park.

Date
Month · Year
Project type
Solo prototypes
Organisation
DigiPen Institute of Technology
My role
Interaction designer and Unity prototyper
Methods used
Unity, HUD design, camera and audio feedback

Reading the state of an attack

The combat prototype takes place in an abandoned carnival. I used gumballs to show the ammo count and carnival tickets for the light, medium and heavy attack modes. The HUD removes gumballs as ammo is spent and refills them on reload.

A run from a dog’s point of view

The park prototype explores the feeling of a dog getting the zoomies. I worked with a low first-person camera, quick movement and responsive sound to make ordinary park features feel close and fast as the player passes them.

What each prototype taught me

In the combat prototype, I used sound and impact cues to tell the player when an attack landed and used the HUD to communicate what was available next. In the park prototype, the camera and audio carry more of the experience. I used each exercise to work out which feedback the player needs in the moment.
