Game Feel in Unity

I built two Unity prototypes around game feel. One uses a carnival HUD to communicate combat state. The other uses a low camera and sound to make a sprint through a park feel like a dog has the zoomies.

Date
Month · Year
Project type
Solo prototypes
My role
Interaction designer and Unity prototyper
Methods used
Unity, HUD design, camera, audio feedback

Combat feedback in an abandoned carnival

Gumballs show how much ammo the player has. Carnival tickets identify light, medium and heavy attacks. The HUD takes gumballs away as the player fires and refills them on reload. I used sound and impact cues to help a player read whether an attack landed.

A park from a dog’s eye level

For the second prototype I used a low first-person camera, quick movement and responsive sound. The camera height makes familiar park features feel close, and the route and sound cues help communicate the speed of the run.

What I learned from the two prototypes

Combat needs to communicate which options are available and whether an action worked. The park experience depends more on speed, scale and proximity. Building both gave me a practical way to explore which feedback carries meaning in each situation.
